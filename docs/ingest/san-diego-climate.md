# San Diego Climate

San Diego is world-famous for its idyllic **Mediterranean climate**, characterized by warm, dry summers and mild winters. It is often cited as having some of the best weather in the United States.

## Weather Phenomena

### "May Gray" and "June Gloom"
In late spring and early summer, a marine layer (low-level clouds) often blankets the coast in the morning.
*   **May Gray:** The onset of this pattern in May.
*   **June Gloom:** The peak of the cloudy pattern in June.
Usually, the clouds burn off by the afternoon, revealing sunny skies, but occasionally they can persist all day at the beaches.

### Santa Ana Winds
A weather condition occurring typically in fall and winter. High pressure builds over the Great Basin, sending hot, dry air flowing westward from the desert towards the coast.
*   These winds can raise temperatures significantly (even in winter) and dramatically lower humidity.
*   They often increase the risk of wildfires due to the extreme dryness.

## Microclimates
Due to its topography of coastline, canyons, mesas, and mountains, San Diego has distinct microclimates.
*   **Coastal:** Mild temperatures year-round, rarely getting very hot or very cold.
*   **Inland Valleys:** (e.g., El Cajon, Escondido) Significantly hotter in the summer and cooler in the winter than the coast.
*   **Mountains/Desert:** The eastern part of the county features high mountains (getting snow in winter) and desert (extreme heat in summer).
