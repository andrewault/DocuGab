# Geography of San Diego

San Diego is situated on the coast of the Pacific Ocean in Southern California, immediately adjacent to the Mexican border. The city lies approximately 120 miles (190 km) south of Los Angeles and is characterized by a unique topography of mesas, canyons, and deep valleys.

## Physical Geography

### Topography
The terrain of San Diego is varied and distinctive, consisting of three primary zones:
1.  **Coastal Plain**: A strip of land along the ocean, featuring beaches and sea cliffs.
2.  **Mesas and Canyons**: Inland from the coast, the land rises into a series of mesas (flat-topped hills) separated by deep, steep-sided canyons. This "mesa and canyon" geography heavily influences the city's urban planning and transportation networks.
3.  **Mountains**: Further east, the terrain rises into the Peninsular Ranges, including the Laguna, Cuyamaca, and Palomar mountains, which reach elevations over 6,000 feet.

### Water Bodies
*   **San Diego Bay**: A natural deep-water harbor protected by the peninsulas of Point Loma and Coronado.
*   **Mission Bay**: A man-made aquatic park used for recreation.
*   **San Diego River**: Running west from the mountains to the Pacific Ocean, dividing the city into northern and southern sections.

## Climate
San Diego enjoys a **Mediterranean climate** (Köppen climate classification *Csa*/*BSh*), characterized by warm, dry summers and mild winters.
*   **Temperature**: Average daily temperatures range from 57°F (14°C) in January to 72°F (22°C) in July.
*   **Precipitation**: Most rainfall occurs between December and March, averaging about 10 inches (254 mm) annually.
*   **Microclimates**: Due to the topography, weather can vary significantly over short distances, with coastal areas being cooler and foggier ("May Gray" and "June Gloom") while inland valleys are hotter and sunnier.

## Ecological Zones
The region supports diverse habitats, primarily **chaparral** and **coastal sage scrub** plant communities, which are drought-resistant and adapted to the semi-arid climate. The Torrey Pine (*Pinus torreyana*), the rarest pine species in the United States, is endemic to the coastal bluffs of northern San Diego.
