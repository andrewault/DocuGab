# Transportation in San Diego

San Diego is connected by a vast network of freeways, public transit, and regional rail systems.

## Public Transit

### San Diego Trolley
Operated by the **Metropolitan Transit System (MTS)**, the Trolley is an iconic bright red light rail system serving many popular areas.
*   **Blue Line:** Runs from the border at San Ysidro up to UTC/UCSD, connecting downtown and Old Town.
*   **Green Line:** Runs from downtown through Mission Valley to Santee, stopping at attractions like Snapdragon Stadium.
*   **Orange Line:** Connects downtown to eastern suburbs like Lemon Grove and El Cajon.

### Buses
MTS also operates a comprehensive bus network covering the city and suburbs, including "Rapid" distinct bus lines that offer faster service with fewer stops.

## Regional Rail

### COASTER
Operated by the North County Transit District (NCTD), this commuter train runs along the breathtaking coastline, connecting downtown San Diego with coastal communities like Solana Beach, Encinitas, Carlsbad, and Oceanside.

### Amtrak Pacific Surfliner
One of the busiest intercity rail routes in the nation, the Surfliner connects San Diego to Los Angeles, Santa Barbara, and San Luis Obispo.

### SPRINTER
A hybrid rail system running east-west in North County, connecting Oceanside to Escondido.

## Major Freeways

San Diego is defined by its canyon-spanning freeway system:
*   **Interstate 5 (I-5):** The main north-south artery running along the coast from Mexico to the Oregon border.
*   **Interstate 8 (I-8):** Runs east-west from Ocean Beach into the desert.
*   **Interstate 15 (I-15):** The inland north-south route.
*   **Interstate 805 (I-805):** A bypass route for the I-5 through the city.
*   **State Route 163 (SR-163):** Famous for its scenic stretch through Balboa Park under the cabaret bridge.
