# Mountains in San Diego County

San Diego County is home to a diverse range of mountains, offering hiking trails with sweeping views of the ocean, city, and desert. The local mountains are part of the Peninsular Ranges, which extend from Southern California down into Baja California.

## Major Peaks

### Cowles Mountain
- **Height:** 1,593 ft
- **Location:** Mission Trails Regional Park
- **Details:** The highest point within the city limits of San Diego. It's an extremely popular hike due to its accessibility and 360-degree views of the county.

### Mount Woodson
- **Height:** 2,894 ft
- **Location:** Poway
- **Details:** Famous for "Potato Chip Rock," a thin sliver of granite that makes for a popular photo opportunity. The trail is steep and exposed but rewarding.

### Iron Mountain
- **Height:** 2,696 ft
- **Location:** Poway
- **Details:** A moderate-to-difficult hike that offers panoramic views. The trail is well-maintained and provides a good workout without being as crowded as Cowles Mountain.

### Cuyamaca Peak
- **Height:** 6,512 ft
- **Location:** Cuyamaca Rancho State Park
- **Details:** The second-highest peak in San Diego County. The trail is paved for most of the way up and offers stunning views of the ocean and, on clear days, islands off the coast.

### Hot Springs Mountain
- **Height:** 6,533 ft
- **Location:** Los Coyotes Indian Reservation
- **Details:** The highest point in San Diego County. Access requires a fee as it is on tribal land, but it offers a secluded and scenic experience.

### Mount Laguna
- **Height:** ~6,000 ft (Monument Peak: 6,271 ft)
- **Location:** Cleveland National Forest
- **Details:** A mountain range rather than a single peak, Mount Laguna offers pine forests and meadows that feel worlds away from the coastal chaparral. It's a popular spot for camping and snow play in the winter.

## Hiking Tips
- **Heat:** Trails can get extremely hot, especially in the summer. Hikes like Cowles and Iron Mountain have very little shade. Bring plenty of water and avoid hiking during the hottest part of the day.
- **Wildlife:** Rattlesnakes are common in the warmer months. Keep an eye on the trail and keep dogs on a leash.
- **Preservation:** Stick to established trails to protect the local vegetation and prevent erosion.
