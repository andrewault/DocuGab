# History of San Diego

## Pre-Colonial Era
The area now known as San Diego was originally inhabited by the Kumeyaay people (also known as the Diegueño), who lived in the region for as many as 12,000 years prior to European contact. They established villages in the valleys and along the coast, relying on a hunter-gatherer lifestyle that utilized the rich resources of the land and sea.

## Spanish Colonization (1542–1821)
The first European to visit the region was Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo, sailing under the flag of Spain, who entered San Diego Bay in 1542. He named the area "San Miguel." It wasn't until 1602 that Sebastián Vizcaíno arrived and renamed the bay "San Diego" in honor of San Didacus of Alcalá.

Permanent European settlement began in 1769 with the arrival of the Gaspar de Portolá expedition. Along with Father Junípero Serra, they established **Mission San Diego de Alcalá**, the first of the 21 California missions. The Presidio of San Diego was built on a hill overlooking the bay to protect the mission, marking the birth of the first European settlement in what is now California.

## Mexican Period (1821–1848)
After Mexico gained independence from Spain in 1821, San Diego became part of the Mexican territory of Alta California. The mission system was secularized in 1833, distributing mission lands to private citizens as "ranchos." During this time, the pueblo of San Diego (now Old Town) grew slowly as a center for the cattle hide and tallow trade.

## American Period (1848–Present)
The United States took control of San Diego in 1848 following the Mexican-American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo. California was admitted to the Union as a state in 1850.

### Alonzo Horton and "New Town"
In 1867, developer Alonzo Horton arrived and purchased 960 acres of land near the waterfront, believing the city center should be closer to the bay. This area, known as "New Town," rapidly developed into what is today **Downtown San Diego**, eclipsing the original Old Town settlement.

### Military and Aviation
San Diego's strategic location led to a significant military presence. The U.S. Navy established a coaling station in 1901, and the city became a major naval base, especially during World War II. The city is also known as the birthplace of naval aviation.

In the 20th century, San Diego became a hub for the aerospace and defense industries, hosting companies like Convair.

## Modern Era
Today, San Diego is known for its mild climate, extensive beaches, and as a center for biotechnology, healthcare, and international trade. It remains a major military hub and a popular tourist destination, home to attractions like Balboa Park and the San Diego Zoo.
